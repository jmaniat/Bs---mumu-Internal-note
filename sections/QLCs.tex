\subsection{Quark Level Corrections (QLCs)}
\label{sec:QLCs}

The nominal set of MC samples used in the analysis have two different types of cuts applied in their generation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Quark Level Cuts}: These are cuts applied on the $b$-quark kinematics
    \item \textit{Final State Cuts}: These are cuts applied on the final state objects, e.g. muons.  
\end{itemize}
The Quark Level Corrections (QLCs) correct for the bias of the quark level cuts, and are evaluated using dedicated sets of MC samples; one with the same quark-level cuts as used in the nominal samples, the other with significantly looser quark-level cuts.  
No cuts on final state objects are applied to either set of MC samples for the QLC evaluation.
As in the PR2 analysis, the QLCs are computed in bins of $pT_B$, $\eta_B$ as  
\begin{equation}
 W_{\text{QL}} = \nu^{\text{FScuts}}_{\text{quarkBiased}} \cdot \left(  \frac{\sigma^{\text{Pythia}}_{\text{quarkBiased}}}{N^{\text{tot}}_{\text{quarkBiased}}} \right) / \left[ \nu^{\text{FScuts}}_{\text{unbiased}} \cdot \left( \frac{\sigma^{\text{Pythia}}_{\text{unbiased}}}{N^{\text{tot}}_{\text{unbiased}}} \right) \right],
\label{eq:QLCformula}
\end{equation}
where $\nu$ is the number of entries in a ($pT_B$, $\eta_B$) bin from 
the unbiased or quark biased samples after applying the final state 
particle cuts. $\sigma^{\text{Pythia}}$ and $N^{\text{tot}}$ are, respectively, the Pythia-calculated generation cross-section and the number of generated events for the given samples. 
The $\frac{\sigma^{\text{Pythia}}}{N^{\text{tot}}}$ terms in equation~\ref{eq:QLCformula} account for the relative 'integrated luminosity' of the two MC samples considered.


QLCs are evaluated separately for the \Bmumu, \BpmKpmJpsi and \BsJpsiPhi processes. 
In the case of the \Bmumu samples, the same job options as in the PR2 analysis are used thus the QLCs do not need to be re-evaluated. 
For the \BpmKpmJpsi and \BsJpsiPhi samples, the nominal MC samples have looser quark-level cuts then was used in the PR2 analysis, resulting in less extreme  values of the corrections (i.e. values closer to 1.0).
