\section{\Bs and \Bd Signal Extraction}
\label{sec:SignalExtraction}

In order to extract the \Bds signal yields, an extended simultaneous unbinned maximum-likelihood fit is performed on the invariant mass distribution of \Bds events that are classified according to different intervals in the BDT output. This strategy is similar to the one followed by \lhcb \cite{LHCb:2021vsc} and \cms \cite{CMS:2022mgd}, along with the \atlas Partial Run 2 version of the analysis \cite{ATLAS:2018cur}. Unless otherwise stated, throughout this chapter we assume the latest SM branching fraction predictions \BRBdmumu = $3.66 \times 10^{-9}$ and \BRBsmumu = $1.03 \times 10^{-10}$ as mentioned in Section~\ref{sec:intro}. To prevent introduction of biases or double counting effects during the development of the fitting procedure, MC samples have been used for preparatory studies and where possible verified on data sidebands.

The continuum BDT output bin boundaries used in this analysis have been selected to correspond to 18\% signal efficiency in each of the BDT bins with increasing signal to background ratio for higher BDT output bins, similar to the PR2 analysis \cite{ATLAS:2018cur}. A dedicated study was performed to identify potential improvements to the signal sensitivity by considering alternative number of BDT bins and signal efficiencies in these bins. In this study, 10,000 toy MC samples were generated and fit with the baseline fit model (described in detail in section~\ref{subsec:FitConfig}) for each of the BDT bin configurations considered. The average fit error on the \BRBsmumu and \BRBdmumu were compared to the 4 BDT bin equal signal efficiency errors. Table~\ref{tab:BDTbinningResults} shows the result of a subset of tested fit configurations (the full list of which can be found in Appendix~\textcolor{red}{Add appenfix for this}).

For the 3 and 4 BDT bin fit configurations the most signal sensitive (top) BDT bin boundary has been adjusted to include a 20\% and 50\% larger/smaller signal efficiency respectively. This was done to evaluate the change in signal sensitivity to variations in BDT boundary selection for the most signal sensitive bin. In both these cases the signal sensitivity was not found to vary significantly. Overall the 5 equal signal efficiency BDT bin configuration showed the best sensitivity to both the signals, however, this improvement is still negligible with respect to the expected statistical uncertainty on the signal branching ratios. With this consideration, the fact that a reasonably simple model is required for the complex fit on data, and for consistency with the PR2 BDT binning strategy, the equal 18\% signal efficiency 4 BDT bin configuration is chosen for this analysis. \subsection{Signal Models}
\label{subsec:SignalModels}

The \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu signals are both modelled by a superposition of two Gaussians as shown in equation~\ref{eq:DoubleGaussian}
\begin{eqnarray}
  \hbox{\hspace{-16mm}}F(x) = f \cdot G(x; \mu_1, \sigma_1) + (1 - f) \cdot G(x; \mu_2, \sigma_2) =
  \hbox{\hspace{-0mm}}f \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_1} \exp\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2}\right] + (1-f) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\
      \pi}\sigma_2} \exp\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2}\right]
  \label{eq:DoubleGaussian} 
\end{eqnarray}
, where to take into account the radiative tails of these signals the means of the two gaussians ($\mu_1$,$\mu_2$) are modelled independently. The other parameters in equation~\ref{eq:DoubleGaussian} include the independent sigmas of the two Gaussians ($\sigma_1$,$\sigma_2$), as well as the fraction of first Gaussian with respect to the second Gaussian ($f$). To extract the shape parameters of these distribution the signal MC is fit with the signal model. It was found that the shape parameters are not dependent on the BDT output as expected.

Both the \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu MC samples of O($10^5$) events used in this study are significantly larger than the statistics expected in data of O(300) for \Bsmumu and O(30) for \Bdmumu. Due to the large MC sample size the fit would be sensitive to subtle details in the PDF shape, which are completely irrelevant for the fit to data where these details are indistinguishable due to the limited statistics. For this reason the fit is performed on bootstrapped MC samples where the bootstrapping procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Separate the signal MC into 4 subsamples for each of the 4 BDT bins,
    \item From each of the subsamples select 4000 events at random to create four bootstrapped MC subsamples,
    \item Simultaneously fit the 4 bootstrapped MC subsamples with a constraint that the shape is the same across all BDT bins,
    \item Repeat the procedure 1000 times,
    \item Calculate the average of the shape parameters from the 1000 repitions of the simultaneous bootstrapped MC fits.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Resonant Background Model}
\label{subsec:ResonantBkgModel}

The resonant background consists of \Bhh decays such as \BKpKm, \BKpmpimp, and \Bpippim, where both hadrons are misidentified as muons. Due to the misreconstruction of $K \to \mu$, and to a smaller extent $\pi \to \mu$, the peak of the \Bhh decays is shifted to lower masses and the their mass distribution becomes highly superimposed with the \Bd signal.

To accurately model the \Bhh background and extract the data-driven normalisation for this crucial to the final fit background component, trigger-level and mass-level corrections are necessary to be applied to the \Bhh MC samples. These corrections account for the difference in trigger efficiencies and invariant mass reconstruction between real muons and hadron fakes, ensuring realistic treatment of this backgrounds contribution in the signal region.

\subsubsection{\Bhh Normalisation}
\label{subsubsec:BhhNormalisation}

To get a realistic measurement of the \Bd signal yield, which could otherwise be unrealistically inflated (or deflated) by the \Bhh and \Bdmumu invariant mass overlap when fitted to data, the \Bhh normalisation will be externally constrained in the final fit to data and hence a realistic estimate of the resonant background yield is required.

SM predicted estimates can be calculated by considering the \Bhh equivalent of the master euqation~\ref{eq:BRFormula}, rearranged for the dimuon yield and using the latest measurements of the \Bhh branching ratios from PDG \cite{ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk}. Such predictions have been calculated by incorporating the the estimated \Bpm  yield of Chapter~\ref{sec:ReferenceChannel}, ratio of acceptance times efficiency from Chapter~\ref{sec:AccepEffRatio}, and using the fake rates from Chapter~\ref{sec:FakeBackground} \textcolor{red}{Dont forget to update these chapters to sections where the numbers are. For now PR2 fakes rates are being used}.

Table~\ref{tab:MasterEqEstMuonWPs} shows the SM predictions for \Bmumu and \Bhh yields for all combinations of Tight (T) and Loose (L) muons in the final states. By construction the Loose but Not Tight (LNT) muons pass the L muon WP but fail the T muon WP, and are therefore orthogonal to the T muons. Based upon these yields, we fit the LNT-LNT data region with the adjusted version of the baseline model described in section~\ref{subsec:FitConfig}. The LNT-LNT region provides the best environment to extract the \Bhh yield due to its higher fraction of events with respect to the \Bs and \Bd than the LNT-T or the T-LNT regions. The adjustments to the fit model include:
\begin{itemize}
\item Fixing the \Bs (\Bd) model parameters to the result of the simultaneous extended unbinned maximum likelihood fit in the LNT-LNT region \Bs (\Bd) MC sample to get a more suitable representation of the signal shape in this orthogonal to the signal region,
\item Fixing the \Bhh shape parameters to the result of the corrected LNT-LNT \Bhh MC fit in Chapter~\ref{sec:FakeBackground} \textcolor{red}{change this to the actual section once written},
\item Fixing the non-resonant background model parameters to the result of the data sidebands fit in the T-T region in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB},
\item Leaving the non-resonant background components normalisations free,
\item Fixing the signal yields to the simulation-based master equation estimates of table~\ref{tab:MasterEqEstMuonWPs}

\end{itemize}

The fit quality for low statistic cases, like the LNT-LNT data fit, is evaluated using a different statistic than the Pearson $\chi^2$, which breaks down at low event counts where the $\chi^{2}$ distribution approximation no longer holds. Instead, the Baker-Cousins $\chi^{2}$ ($\chi^{2}_{BC}$) statistic is used since it incorporates the exact Poisson likelihood and hence can accomodate the low statistics Poisson regime. However, the $\chi^{2}_{BC}$ does not follow standard $\chi^{2}$ distribution with a well defined number of degrees of freedom so analytical methods of approximating the p-value with this test statistic are unsuitable. Instead we resort to the frequentist toy MC based approach in calculation of the p-value. The p-value for the goodness of fit is calculated as the fraction of toy MC datasets generated according to the fitted model that provide a $\chi^{2}_{BC}$ that is higher than or equal to the one observed in the fit to the original dataset. This method will hence forth be used in the goodness of fit p-value evaluations of low statistic sample fits due to its reliability in such regimes.

To get a prediction in the T-T signal region to be used as a constraint on the \Bhh yield in the full baseline model intended on data, we translate the LNT-LNT data \Bhh yield into the T-T region using a ratio of counted \Bhh MC events in the full fit BDT range using all available events including those outside the mass fit range:
\begin{eqnarray}
  N_{hh'}^{T-T} = \frac{MC_{hh'}^{T-T}}{MC_{hh'}^{LNT-LNT}} \times N_{hh'}^{LNT-LNT}
  = (0.64 \pm 0.17) \times (13.6 \pm 5.5) = (8.7 \pm 4.2) \text{ events}.
  \label{eq:T-T_LNT-LNT_dataBhhYield} 
\end{eqnarray}

As a cross-check of the procedure used to translate the LNT-LNT \Bhh yield into the T-T region we also translate the LNT-LNT \Bhh yield into the T-LNT + LNT-T region, which is then compared to the result of a direct fit in the T-LNT + LNT-T data. For the T-LNT + LNT-T data fit the \Bhh yield is constrained to the result of the LNT-LNT data \Bhh yield translated into the T-LNT + LNT-T region. The constraint is therefore:
\begin{eqnarray}
  N_{hh'}^{T-LNT+LNT-T} = \frac{MC_{hh'}^{T-LNT+LNT-T}}{MC_{hh'}^{LNT-LNT}} \times N_{hh'}^{LNT-LNT}
  = (1.46 \pm 0.31) \times (13.6 \pm 5.5) = (19.8 \pm 9.0) \text{ events}.
  \label{eq:T-LNT-LNT-T_LNT-LNT_dataBhhYield} 
\end{eqnarray}

Figure~\ref{fig:T-LNT-LNT-Tdatafit} shows the result of the T-LNT + LNT-T data fit with the constraint on the \Bhh yield from equation~\ref{eq:T-LNT-LNT-T_LNT-LNT_dataBhhYield}. The fitted shape reproduces the data reasonably well with a satisfactory quality of the fit in each BDT bin. The resulting \Bhh yield was found to be $N^{T-LNT+LNT-T}_{hh'} = 8.7 \pm 8.0$ ($32.5 \pm 1.1$ expected). The result of the T-LNT + LNT-T data fit is consistent with the result of the LNT-LNT data fit translated into the T-LNT + LNT-T region within the uncertainties on these values.

\subsection{Non-Resonant Background Components Models}
\label{subsec:NonResonantBkgModel}

The main source of background in the signal region are the \bb decays to two or more muons, with prompt contributions suppressed significantly by the BDT output selection. \textcolor{red}{Add a couple sentences about the confirmation of this in data sidebands, and give the purity comparing to the PR2 analysis.}

The large \bbmumuX MC sample is used to study the different non-resonant background contributions to the signal channel. The truth information from the \bbmumuX sample allows to classify the sources of this background according to their origin with the following classifications:
\begin{itemize}
   \item \textbf{Combinatorial} background, due to muons from opposite sides of the \bb legs. This is the dominant background contribution that is highly suppressed by the BDT 
   \item \textbf{Same-Side (SS)} background, due to muons originating by the same \bQuark leg from cascades \ensuremath{b\to c\mu^{-}\nu\to s(c)\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\nu}   
   \item \textbf{Same-Vertex (SV)} background, due to partially reconstructed \bmeson decays containing muon pairs like \BpmKmumu
   \item \textbf{\Bc} background, mostly due to \ensuremath{B^{\pm}\to\mu^{±}\nu J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})} 
   \item \textbf{\bQuark-Onia} background, due to heavier \bb bound states such as \UpsilonMeson decaying to two muons and photons
   \item \textbf{Unmatched} background, which are events that could not be associated to MC truth that are coming from any underlying physics processes or pile-up vertices. This classification is very small compared to the combinatorial background and hence is merged with it. 
\end{itemize}

Separately studied on exclusive MC samples are the semileptonic decays \AntiLPMuNu, \LPMuNu, \BsKmMupNu, and \BdPimMuPNu, for which the final state hadron can be misidentified as a muon. Furthermore, at the limit of neutrinos carrying a low fraction of \Bpt, the di-muon invariant mass of the candidate can occupy the signal region.

Figure~\ref{fig:BDTDistrbbmumuX} shows the combinatorial background dominating the low BDT values, whereas the SS+SV and to a less extent the \Bc backgrounds become more important at higher BDT values where the signal will be extracted in the final fit. The \bQuark-Onia background is completely eliminated by the BDT selection in the higher BDT bins, however because a small contribution is still present in the lowest BDT bin this component is merged with the SS+SV background.

The signal extraction strategy is supported by these considerations:
\begin{itemize}
   \item The combinatorial background can be significantly reduced in the high BDT bins by the multivariate selection, and its mild di-muon invariant mass dependence facilitates the interpolation from the mass sidebands into the signal mass region.
   \item When considering the full mass interval the SS+SV background becomes more relevant at high BDT bins, however this background is only present in the low-mass region and hence not relevant in the \Bdmumu and \Bsmumu signal mass region.
   \item The \Bc background behaves similarly to the combinatorial background in mass and to the SS+SV background in BDT, but due to its smaller cross section its presence is less relevant and it is assumed to be absorbed by the SS+SV background as discussed in section~\ref{subsubsec:BcBkg}.   
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Semi-leptonic Backgrounds}
\label{subsubsec:SemilepBkg}

The semi-leptonic background originates from few-body semi-leptonic \B-decays where in the limit of low energy neutrinos a hadron can fake a muon ($h\rightarrow\mu$): the main contributions to this background are the \BdPiMuNu and the \BsKMuNu decays. Also contributing to this background are the \LPMuNu (and its charge conjugate \AntiLPMuNu) decays, for which the mass distribution extends closer to the signal region. Together these semi-leptonic modes are expected to contribute significantly less than the second largest contribution to the total background (SS+SV decays), hence the BDT output bin content for these decays is expected to also be very low.

\textcolor{red}{Describe the procedure to get estimation of the semileptonic yields from the fake rates.}

With these few events expected to enter in the fit region we assume that the exponential (SS+SV) and Chebychev first degree polynomial (combinatorial) PDFs to absorb this contribution, so no explicit semi-leptonic component PDF is added to the default fit model. This assumption is taken into account in the evaluation of the fit systematic uncertainties in section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}. To evaluate the impact of this fit systematic by including the semileptonic PDF in the full model, a functional form for this component is needed. The semi-leptonic decays are modelled by an exponential plus a gaussian tail as shown in equation~\ref{eq:expGaussTail}
\begin{eqnarray}
  F(x) = f \cdot E(x; \alpha) + (1 - f) \cdot G(x; \mu, \sigma)  
       = f \cdot \text{exp}(\alpha x) + (1 - f) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \text{exp}\left[-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right]
  \label{eq:expGaussTail}
\end{eqnarray}
, where $\alpha$ is the exponential constant, $f$ is the fraction of the exponetial with respect to the Gaussian, and where $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are resepctively the mean and sigma of the Gaussian.

To validate the chosen PDF for the semi-leptonic background we fit the dimuon VTX mass distribution from the merged exclusive MC samples for the three modes with the PDF in equation~\ref{eq:expGaussTail}. The ID-based VTX mass is used instead of the MUCALC mass since fake muons frequently fail to produce combined muon tracks which are required in the baseline selection, leading to reduced statistics. Applied to the semil-leptonic MC sample is a loosened selection: all final selections apart from the muon quality requirements and passing the online trigger are applied to the merged MC sample to allow for more statistics necessary for a good quality fit and convergence. Loosening the selections on the semi-leptonic MC to extract the shape parameters for this background is justified because this PDF is only used in evaluation of the the fit systematics and is not intended for the final fit on data.

Figure~\ref{fig:semilepMCfitLoose} shows the loosened selection MC fit results with the fitted PDF in all BDT output bins satisfactory reproducing the shape of the MC sample as can be seen from the residual distributions. With enough statistics the fit quality is also satisfactory with $p$-value > 0.05 in each bin of the BDT output. The loose MC fitted shape parameter values are then fixed in the fit to the full selection merged semi-leptonic MC sample as an extra step of validation: the shape from the loosened selection MC fit must be compatible with the full selection MC fit to validate this approach and confirm that the shape from loose selection also applies to full selection MC.

\subsection{Non-Resonant Background Fits to MC and Data Sidebands}
\label{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB}

The non-resonant backgrounds in the fit to data are intended to be parametrised by an exponential (SS+SV background in the low-mass region) plus a first oder Chebychev polynomial (slowly falling combinatorial background in the full mass fit range). First we validate the combination of the chosen functional forms in section~\ref{subsubsec:SSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB}, after which we validate the additional constraints on the exponential and polynomial slopes (arising from introducing the BDT-simultaneous fit strategy) in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB}. In both these validations the MC fit results are compared to those from data by performing the relevant fits on the mass sidebands defined in section~\ref{sec:StrategyOverview}.

\subsection{Summary of the Fit Configuration}
\label{subsec:FitConfig}

The baseline model is a simultaneous PDF , with individual full model PDFs for each of the 4 BDT bins. For each of the simultaneous full model PDFs the following components and their PDFs are included:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Signal PDFs: The \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu signals are each described by a separate double Gaussian PDF in dimuon invariant mass. The values for the two means, two sigmas, and the fraction of Gaussian 1 with repsect to Gaussian 2 are fixed to the result of the bootstrapped MC study for \Bsmumu PDF in section~\ref{subsubsec:BsmumuModel} and separately for \Bdmumu PDF in section~\ref{subsubsec:BdmumuModel}. The number of signal events in each BDT bin is defined to contain a fraction of the total signal yield in the signal BDT bins via the parameterisation $N_{\text{Bin}} = f_{\text{Bin}}\times N_{\text{Tot}}$. The fractions of events are all designed to contain 1/3 of the total number of events in the signal BDT bins. In the top two BDT bins the fractions of events are Gaussian constrained to the systematic uncertainty on the relative fractions of signal in the BDT bins derrived in section \textcolor{red}{Add the section here}; the first bin is defined as a derrived parameter such that $f_{\text{Bin1}} = 1 - f_{\text{Bin2}} - f_{\text{Bin3}}$. Due to the definition of the BDT bins to each contain an equal number of signal events, and because in BDT bin 0 the signal content was found not to increase the signal sensitivity, the fraction of signal events in BDT bin 0 is fixed for consistency to the average content of the signal BDT bins (1/3). The total signal yields are initialised to the SM prediction based off the master equation~\ref{eq:BRFormula}. Two additional smearing parameters describing the correlated systematic error in the fit to the number of signal events are included in the model, discussed in detail in section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}.
\item Resonant background PDF: The \Bhh background is described by a double Gaussian PDF in dimuon invariant mass. The shape parameters are fixed to the result of the fit to corrected \Bhh MC as shown in section~\ref{subsubsec:BhhModel}. The normalisation of the \Bhh PDF in the signal BDT bins is defined to contain exactly 1/3 of the total number of \Bhh events, with the fraction of events in BDT bin 0 fixed to the average of the other three (1/3) for consistency. The total \Bhh yield is initialised to the result of the data-driven \Bhh yield estimate extrapolated from LNT-LNT data to the signal region using MC counting approach discussed in detail in section~\ref{subsubsec:BhhNormalisation}. A Gaussian constraint is applied on the total \Bhh normalisation to incorporate the systematic effect from the estimate derrivation fit strategy and the muon fake-rate probabilities.
\item SSSV background PDF: The SSSV background is described by a falling exponential PDF in dimuon invariant mass. Due to the observations made in section~\ref{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB}, the shape is assumed unifrom across all BDT bins. This assumption is addressed in the systematics study of section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}. The SSSV background normalisations are defined separately in each BDT bin as free parameters, with initial values from the result of the data sidebands combined background model fit in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB} extrapolated into the full mass fit range.
\item Combinatorial background PDF: The combinatorial background is described by a $1^{\text{st}}$ order Chebychev polynomial PDF in dimuon invariant mass. Due to the observations made in section~\ref{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB} the slopes of the Chebychev polynomials in each BDT bin are constrained to a line as a function of the average BDT output of the events in the corresponding BDT bin such that $\alpha_{\text{Chebychev}}^{\text{Bin}} = p0 + p1 \times \text{BDT}_{\text{avg.}}^{\text{Bin}}$. This assumption is addressed in the systematics study of section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}. The combinatorial background normalisations are defined separately in each BDT bin as free parameters, with initial values from the result of the data sidebands combined background model fit in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB} extrapolated into the full mass fit range.
\end{enumerate}
