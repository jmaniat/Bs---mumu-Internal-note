\section{\Bs and \Bd Signal Extraction}
\label{sec:SignalExtraction}

To extract the \Bds signal yields, an extended simultaneous unbinned maximum-likelihood fit is performed on the invariant mass distribution of \Bds events in different intervals in the BDT output, after applying the selections detailed in Table~\ref{tab:baselineSelection}.
This strategy is similar to the one followed by \lhcb \cite{LHCb:2021vsc} and \cms \cite{CMS:2022mgd}, along with the \atlas Partial Run 2 version of the analysis \cite{ATLAS:2018cur}. Unless otherwise stated, throughout this chapter we assume the latest SM branching fraction predictions \BRBdmumu = $3.66 \times 10^{-9}$ and \BRBsmumu = $1.03 \times 10^{-10}$ as mentioned in Section~\ref{sec:intro}. To prevent introduction of biases or double counting effects during the development of the fitting procedure, MC samples have been used for preparatory studies and where possible verified on data sidebands.

In this section, a summary of the background models is first provided after which the derivation of the BDT bin boundaries is discussed followed by details of the functional forms of the signal and background components and sensitivity studies are presented. 
Specifically, there are the following subsections:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An overview of the signal and background processes and how they are modelled or accounted for in the signal channel fit is provided for in Section~\ref{subsec:ProcessSummary}. 
    \item The optimisation of the BDT bin boundaries is discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:BDTbins} 
    \item The \Bds signal models are discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:SignalModels} 
    \item The resonant \Bhh background model is discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:ResonantBkgModel}
    \item The non-resonant background models are discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:NonResonantBkgModel}
    \item Fits of the non-resonant backgrounds to data and MC sidebands are discussed in Section~\ref{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB}.
    %\item The BDT-simultaneous non-resonant backgrounds combined fits to MC and data sidebands comparison is discussed in Section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB}
    \item A summary of the baseline fit model is provided in Section~\ref{subsec:FitConfig}
    \item The studies of the sensitivity to the signal is shown in Section~\ref{subsec:ToyExperimentFits}
    \item Systematic uncertainties on the fit are discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Overview of Signal and Background Processes}
\label{subsec:ProcessSummary}
In each BDT bin in the signal channel the following processes are modelled explicitly:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu signal processes, each modelled with a double Gaussian. 
  \item The Same-Side Same-Vertex (SSSV) background: partially reconstructed candidates with two real muons that originate from the same $b$-quark fragmentation (in some instances the same vertex). This background is situated at lower mass values than the signals and is modelled using a decaying exponential function.   
  \item The Combinatorial background: originating from candidates in which the muons come from different $b/\bar{b}$-quark fragmentations. Although greatly reduced by the BDT output requirement, this background is still present in the SR. Its invariant mass distribution is relatively flat across the range considered here and as such it is modelled using a first order Chebyshev polynomial. 
  \item The Resonant Background due to \Bhh: Consisting of candidates with two fake muons as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:FakeBackground}. This background peaks at invariant masses slightly lower than the \Bmumu signals. This background is also modelled with a double Gaussian but has its normalisation constrained, as discussed in section~\ref{subsec:BhhNormalisation}. 
\end{itemize}

For illustration, an example fit to a toy pseudo-experiment can be seen in Figure~\ref{fig:ToyMCbaseline}.
On top of these processes, two additional backgrounds are considered but are not modelled explicitly, the lack of explicit modelling instead being treated as systematic uncertainties. 
Specifically, the following processes are considered:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \Bc background, mainly due to $B_c^+\to \mu^{+}\nu + J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})$ decays.  
  \item Backgrounds due to semi-leptonic decays in which the $B$-candidate is formed from one real muon and one fake muon. In particular the decays \AntiLPMuNu, \LPMuNu, \BsKmMupNu, and \BdPimMuPNu are considered. 
\end{itemize} 
Both of these backgrounds have a decaying exponential shape and are expected to be sufficiently small such that they can be safely absorbed into the SSSV exponential. 
This is tested using toy psuedo-experiments discussed in section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}

\begin{itemize}

\item Bin 3: 0.4055 - 1.0000,

\subsection{Signal Models}
\label{subsec:SignalModels}

The \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu signals are both modelled by a superposition of two Gaussian distributions as shown in equation~\ref{eq:DoubleGaussian}
\begin{eqnarray}
  \hbox{\hspace{-16mm}}F(x) = f \cdot G(x; \mu_1, \sigma_1) + (1 - f) \cdot G(x; \mu_2, \sigma_2) =
  \hbox{\hspace{-0mm}}f \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_1} \exp\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2}\right] + (1-f) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\
      \pi}\sigma_2} \exp\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2}\right],
  \label{eq:DoubleGaussian} 
\end{eqnarray}
where, to take into account the radiative tails of these signals, the means of the two gaussians ($\mu_1$,$\mu_2$) are modelled independently. The other parameters in equation~\ref{eq:DoubleGaussian} include the independent sigmas of the two Gaussians ($\sigma_1$,$\sigma_2$), as well as the fraction of first Gaussian with respect to the second Gaussian ($f$). To extract the shape parameters of these distributions the signal \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu samples are each fit with this model. 
It was tested that, as in the Partial Run 2 analysis, the shape parameters are not dependent on the BDT output.

Both the \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu MC samples are of O($10^5$), significantly larger than the statistics expected in data of O(300) for \Bsmumu and O(30) for \Bdmumu. 
Due to the large MC sample size the fit would be sensitive to subtle details in the PDF shape, which are irrelevant for the fit to data where these details are indistinguishable due to the limited statistics. For this reason the fit is performed on bootstrapped MC samples where the bootstrapping procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Separate the signal MC into 4 subsamples for each of the 4 BDT bins,
    \item From each of the subsamples select 4000 events at random to create four bootstrapped MC subsamples,
    \item Simultaneously fit the 4 bootstrapped MC subsamples with a constraint that the shape is the same across all BDT bins,
    \item Repeat the procedure 1000 times,
    \item Calculate the average of the shape parameters from the 1000 repetitions of the simultaneous bootstrapped MC fits.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Non-Resonant Background Components Models}
\label{subsec:NonResonantBkgModel}

The main source of background in the signal region are the \bb decays to two or more muons, with prompt contributions suppressed significantly by the BDT output selection. \textcolor{red}{Add a couple sentences about the confirmation of this in data sidebands, and give the purity comparing to the PR2 analysis.}

The large \bbmumuX MC sample is used to study the different non-resonant background contributions to the signal channel. The truth information from the \bbmumuX sample allows classification of the sources of this background according to their origin in the following categories:
\begin{itemize}
   \item \textbf{Combinatorial} background, due to muons from opposite sides of the \bb legs. This is the dominant background contribution that is highly suppressed by the BDT 
   \item \textbf{Same-Side (SS)} background, due to muons originating by the same \bQuark leg from cascades \ensuremath{b\to c\mu^{-}\nu\to s(c)\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\nu}   
   \item \textbf{Same-Vertex (SV)} background, due to partially reconstructed \bmeson decays containing muon pairs like \BpmKmumu
   \item \textbf{\Bc} background, mostly due to $B_c^+\to \mu^{+}\nu + J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})$ 
   \item \textbf{\bQuark-Onia} background, due to heavier \bb bound states such as \UpsilonMeson decaying to two muons and photons
   \item \textbf{Unmatched} background, which are events that could not be associated to MC truth that are coming from any underlying physics processes or pile-up vertices. This classification is very small compared to the combinatorial background and hence is merged with it. 
\end{itemize}

Separately studied on exclusive MC samples are the semileptonic decays \AntiLPMuNu, \LPMuNu, \BsKmMupNu, and \BdPimMuPNu, for which the final state hadron can be misidentified as a muon. Furthermore, at the limit of neutrinos carrying a low fraction of \Bpt, the di-muon invariant mass of the candidate can occupy the signal region.

   \item When considering the full mass interval the SS+SV background becomes more relevant at high BDT bins, however this background is only present in the low-mass region and \DJcomment{hence not relevant}{Think this needs to be rephased, I guess the point is that it's ok to determine the shape just from the lower side band?} in the \Bdmumu and \Bsmumu signal mass region.

\subsubsection{SS+SV Background}
\label{subsubsec:SSSVbkg}

The SS+SV background falls fast with di-muon invariant mass, and therefore peaks far below the signal region, however due to detector resolution effects and kinematic limits the analysis is sensitive to the tail of this background. This background component is modelled by an exponential PDF in di-muon invariant mass shown in equation~\ref{eq:SSSVpdf}
\begin{eqnarray}
  F(x) = \text{exp}(\alpha x), 
  \label{eq:SSSVpdf}
\end{eqnarray}

\subsection{Non-Resonant Background Fits to MC and Data Sidebands}
\label{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB}

Having established the functional forms of the dominant non-resonant background components, these functional forms can be tested on data and inclusive MC using the sidebands either side of the tight mass window. 
As a quick summary of the previous section, the non-resonant backgrounds in the fit to data are intended to be parametrised by an exponential (SS+SV background in the low-mass region) plus a first order Chebychev polynomial (slowly falling combinatorial background in the full mass fit range). 
The combination of the chosen functional forms is first validated in each of the BDT bins in section~\ref{subsubsec:SSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB}, after which it is also tested whether these models work when the BDT bins are fit simultaneously in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB}. 
In both these validations the MC fit results are compared to those from data by performing the relevant fits on the mass sidebands defined in section~\ref{sec:StrategyOverview}.

\subsection{Summary of the Fit Configuration}
\label{subsec:FitConfig}

The baseline model is a simultaneous PDF, with individual full model PDFs for each of the 4 BDT bins. For each BDT bin, the following components are included:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Signal PDFs: The \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu signals are each described by a separate double Gaussian PDF in dimuon invariant mass. The values for the two means, two sigmas, and the fraction of Gaussian 1 with repsect to Gaussian 2 are fixed to the result of the bootstrapped MC study for \Bsmumu PDF in section~\ref{subsubsec:BsmumuModel} and separately for \Bdmumu PDF in section~\ref{subsubsec:BdmumuModel}. The number of signal events in each BDT bin is defined to contain a fraction of the total signal yield in the signal BDT bins via the parameterisation $N_{\text{Bin}} = f_{\text{Bin}}\times N_{\text{Tot}}$. The fractions of events are all designed to contain 1/3 of the total number of events in the signal BDT bins. In the top two BDT bins the fractions of events are Gaussian constrained to the systematic uncertainty on the relative fractions of signal in the BDT bins derived in section \textcolor{red}{Add the section here}. The fraction in the first bin is defined as a derived parameter such that $f_{\text{Bin1}} = 1 - f_{\text{Bin2}} - f_{\text{Bin3}}$. Due to the definition of the BDT bins to each contain an equal number of signal events, and because in BDT bin 0 the signal content was found not to increase the signal sensitivity, the fraction of signal events in BDT bin 0 is fixed for consistency to the average content of the signal BDT bins (1/3). The total signal yields are initialised to the SM prediction based off the master equation~\ref{eq:BRFormula}. Two additional smearing parameters describing the correlated systematic error in the fit on the number of signal events are included in the model. These are discussed in detail in section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}.
\item Resonant background PDF: The \Bhh background is described by a double Gaussian PDF in dimuon invariant mass. The shape parameters are fixed to the result of the fit to corrected \Bhh MC as shown in section~\ref{subsec:ResonantBkgModel}. The normalisation of the \Bhh PDF in the signal BDT bins is defined to contain exactly 1/3 of the total number of \Bhh events, with the fraction of events in BDT bin 0 fixed to the average of the other three (1/3) for consistency. The total \Bhh yield is initialised to the result of the data-driven \Bhh yield estimate extrapolated from LNT-LNT data to the signal region using MC counting approach discussed in detail in section~\ref{subsec:BhhNormalisation}. A Gaussian constraint is applied on the total \Bhh normalisation to incorporate its uncertainty.
\item SSSV background PDF: The SSSV background is described by a falling exponential PDF in dimuon invariant mass. Due to the observations made in section~\ref{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB}, the shape is taken to be uniform across all BDT bins. This assumption is addressed in the systematics study of section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}. The SSSV background normalisations are defined separately in each BDT bin as free parameters, with initial values from the result of the data sidebands combined background model fit in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB} extrapolated into the full mass fit range.
\item Combinatorial background PDF: The combinatorial background is described by a $1^{\text{st}}$ order Chebychev polynomial PDF in dimuon invariant mass. Due to the observations made in section~\ref{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB} the slopes of the Chebychev polynomials in each BDT bin are constrained to have a linear dependence on of the average BDT output in each BDT bin such that $\alpha_{\text{Chebychev}}^{\text{Bin}} = p0 + p1 \times \text{BDT}_{\text{avg.}}^{\text{Bin}}$. This assumption is addressed in the systematics study of section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}. The combinatorial background normalisations are defined separately in each BDT bin as free parameters, with initial values from the result of the data sidebands combined background model fit in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB} extrapolated into the full mass fit range.
\end{enumerate}
