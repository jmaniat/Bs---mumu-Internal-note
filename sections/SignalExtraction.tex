\section{\Bs and \Bd Signal Extraction}
\label{sec:SignalExtraction}

In order to extract the \Bds signal yields, an extended simultaneous unbinned maximum-likelihood fit is performed on the invariant mass distribution of \Bds events that are classified according to different intervals in the BDT output. This strategy is similar to the one followed by \lhcb \cite{LHCb:2021vsc} and \cms \cite{CMS:2022mgd}, along with the \atlas Partial Run 2 version of the analysis \cite{ATLAS:2018cur}. Unless otherwise stated, throughout this chapter we assume the latest SM branching fraction predictions \BRBdmumu = $3.66 \times 10^{-9}$ and \BRBsmumu = $1.03 \times 10^{-10}$ as mentioned in Section~\ref{sec:intro}. To prevent introduction of biases or double counting effects during the development of the fitting procedure, MC samples have been used for preparatory studies and where possible verified on data sidebands.

\subsection{Signal Models}
\label{subsec:SignalModels}

The \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu signals are both modelled by a superposition of two Gaussians as shown in equation~\ref{eq:DoubleGaussian}
\begin{eqnarray}
  \hbox{\hspace{-16mm}}F(x) = f \cdot G(x; \mu_1, \sigma_1) + (1 - f) \cdot G(x; \mu_2, \sigma_2) =
  \hbox{\hspace{-0mm}}f \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_1} \exp\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2}\right] + (1-f) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\
      \pi}\sigma_2} \exp\left[-\frac{(x-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2}\right]
  \label{eq:DoubleGaussian} 
\end{eqnarray}
, where to take into account the radiative tails of these signals the means of the two gaussians ($\mu_1$,$\mu_2$) are modelled independently. The other parameters in equation~\ref{eq:DoubleGaussian} include the independent sigmas of the two Gaussians ($\sigma_1$,$\sigma_2$), as well as the fraction of first Gaussian with respect to the second Gaussian ($f$). To extract the shape parameters of these distribution the signal MC is fit with the signal model. It was found that the shape parameters are not dependent on the BDT output as expected.

Both the \Bsmumu and \Bdmumu MC samples of O($10^5$) events used in this study are significantly larger than the statistics expected in data of O(400) for \Bsmumu and O(40) for \Bdmumu. Due to the large MC sample size the fit would be sensitive to subtle details in the PDF shape, which are completely irrelevant for the fit to data where these details are indistinguishable due to the limited statistics. For this reason the fit is performed on bootstrapped MC samples where the bootstrapping procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Separate the signal MC into 4 subsamples \textcolor{red}{for each of the 4 BDT bins}
    \item For each of the subsamples select at random 4000 events
    \item Simultaneously fit the 4 bootstrapped MC subsamples with a constraint that the shape is the same across all BDT bins
    \item Repeat the procedure 1000 times
    \item Take the average of the shape parameters from each bootstrap repetition
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Non-Resonant Background Components Models}
\label{subsec:NonResonantBkgModel}

The main source of background in the signal region are the \bb decays to two or more muons, with prompt contributions suppressed significantly by the BDT output selection. \textcolor{red}{Add a couple sentences about the confirmation of this in data sidebands, and give the purity comparing to the PR2 analysis.}

The large \bbmumuX MC sample is used to study the different non-resonant background contributions to the signal channel. The truth information from the \bbmumuX sample allows to classify the sources of this background according to their origin with the following classifications:
\begin{itemize}
   \item \textbf{Combinatorial} background, due to muons from opposite sides of the \bb legs. This is the dominant background contribution that is highly suppressed by the BDT 
   \item \textbf{Same-Side (SS)} background, due to muons originating by the same \bQuark leg from cascades \ensuremath{b\to c\mu^{-}\nu\to s(c)\mu^{-}\mu^{+}\nu}   
   \item \textbf{Same-Vertex (SV)} background, due to partially reconstructed \bmeson decays containing muon pairs like \BpmKmumu
   \item \textbf{\Bc} background, mostly due to \ensuremath{B^{\pm}\to\mu^{±}\nu J/\psi(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})} 
   \item \textbf{\bQuark-Onia} background, due to heavier \bb bound states such as \UpsilonMeson decaying to two muons and photons
   \item \textbf{Unmatched} background, which are events that could not be associated to MC truth that are coming from any underlying physics processes or pile-up vertices. This classification is very small compared to the combinatorial background and hence is merged with it. 
\end{itemize}

Separately studied on exclusive MC samples are the semileptonic decays \AntiLPMuNu, \LPMuNu, \BsKmMupNu, and \BdPimMuPNu, for which the final state hadron can be misidentified as a muon. Furthermore, at the limit of neutrinos carrying a low fraction of \Bpt, the di-muon invariant mass of the candidate can occupy the signal region.

The BDT and invariant mass distributions for the \bbmumuX MC sample compared to the data mass sidebands after applying all the selection cuts \textcolor{red}{Note this is DDW1516 , will need to be updated to new DDW} (except for the BDT selection) is presented in Figure~\ref{fig:BDTMassDistrbbmumuX}. The combinatorial background of Figure~\ref{fig:MassDistrbbmumuX} shows a relatively mild dependence on the di-muon invariant mass \textcolor{red}{(add statement about the consistency of MC with data similar to PR2 int note)}. This trend is described more in detail in section~\ref{subsubsec:COMBbkg}. The other backgrounds have a sharper dependence on the di-muon invariant mass and hence mostly populate the low-mass region. Figure~\ref{fig:BDTDistrbbmumuX} shows the combinatorial background dominating the low BDT values, whereas the SS+SV and to a less extent the \Bc backgrounds become more important at higher BDT values where the signal will be extracted in the final fit. The \bQuark-Onia background is completely eliminated by the BDT selection in the higher BDT bins, however because a small contribution is still present in the lowest BDT bin this component is merged with the SS+SV background.

The signal extraction strategy is supported by these considerations:
\begin{itemize}
   \item The combinatorial background can be significantly reduced in the high BDT bins by the multivariate selection, and its mild di-muon invariant mass dependence facilitates the interpolation from the mass sidebands into the signal mass region.
   \item When considering the full mass interval the SS+SV background becomes more relevant at high BDT bins, however this background is only present in the low-mass region and hence not relevant in the \Bdmumu and \Bsmumu signal mass region.
   \item The \Bc background behaves similarly to the combinatorial background in mass and to the SS+SV background in BDT, but due to its smaller cross section its presence is less relevant and it is assumed to be absorbed by the SS+SV background as discussed in section~\ref{subsubsec:BcBkg}.   
\end{itemize}
The following sections will discuss the parametrisation of these background components, individual fits to MC, and combined fits to MC and data sidebands.


\subsubsection{Combinatorial Background}
\label{subsubsec:COMBbkg}

The combinatorial background is modelled by a first order Chebychev polynomial in di-muon invariant mass as given by the Probability Density Function (PDF) in equation~\ref{eq:FirstOrderCheb}
\begin{eqnarray}
  F(x) = 1 + \alpha T_{1}(x) = 1 + \alpha\cdot x/\text{1200 MeV}
  \label{eq:FirstOrderCheb}
\end{eqnarray}
, where $\alpha$ is the Chebychev constant that is normalised to the mass fit window of 1200 MeV.

Figure~\ref{fig:CombMCfit} shows the fits performed on the combinatorial component of the \bbmumuX MC (including the unmatched decays as discussed in section~\ref{subsec:NonResonantBkgModel} in the four BDT bins intended for the final fit: all the fits show a similar falling behaviour with increasing \B-candidate mass, satisfactorily reproducing the MC shape in the three highest BDT bins as can be seen in the residual distributions. Figure~\ref{fig:CombMCfitBin0} shows that the fit quality is not satisfactory (with a $p$-value = 0.001) only in BDT bin 0, however this was  found to be due to a very large number of fitted events and the fit quality can be improved by splitting this BDT bin into more equal-event BDT bins: this does not imply mismodeling but rather that the statistical test used to evaluate the fit performance behaves differently at increased statistics. Since the BDT bin 0 is primarily used to constrain the background rather than extract the signal, this lower $p$-value is likely a statistical effect rather than fundamental mismodeling and is therefore not an issue.

\subsubsection{SS+SV Background}
\label{subsubsec:SSSVbkg}

The SS+SV background falls fast with di-muon invariant mass, and therefore peaks far below the signal region, however due to detector resolution effects and kinematic limits the analysis is sensitive to the tail of this background. This background component is modelled by an exponential PDF in di-muon invariant mass shown in equation~\ref{eq:SSSVpdf}
\begin{eqnarray}
  F(x) = \text{exp}(\alpha x) 
  \label{eq:SSSVpdf}
\end{eqnarray}
, where $\alpha$ is the exponential constant.

Figure~\ref{fig:sssvMCfit} \textcolor{red}{( or Figure~\ref{fig:sssvOnlyMCfit} if we choose to only show the sssv part of \bbmumuX MC without \bQuark-Onia or \Bc background samples absorbed)} shows the fits performed independently on the SS+SV component of the \bbmumuX MC in the four BDT bins intended for the final fit: all the fits show a similar sharply falling behaviour with increasing \B-candidate mass, satisfactorily reproducing the MC shape as shown in the residual distributions. The quality of the fit performed in the lower two BDT bins is not satisfactory, however this was found to improve when splitting these BDT bins into more equal-event BDT bins. Similarly to the case of fit quality for low BDT bin fit in section~\ref{subsubsec:COMBbkg}, these lower $p$-value are likely a statistical effect rather than fundamental mismodeling and since the lower BDT bins have lower sensitivity to the signal this is not an issue.

\subsubsection{\Bc Background}
\label{subsubsec:BcBkg}

The \Bc background is a much smaller component with respect to the SS+SV and the combinatorial backgrounds: in the four highest BDT bins the number of expected \Bc events in MC corresponds to 0.9\% (23.8\%), 1.7\% (10.8\%), 2.3\% (5.5\%), and 2.0\% (2.8\%) of the total (or SS+SV + \bQuark-Onia + \Bc) background in BDT bin 0 , BDT bin 1, BDT bin 2, and BDT bin 3 respectively \textcolor{red}{(change these to the ratios from the MC when the final DDW are applied)}. The di-muon mass distribution for \Bc decays peaks below the signal region similar to the SS+SV background, and is modelled by the same exponential PDF as shown in equation~\ref{eq:SSSVpdf}.

This is entirely due to a single (lowest) mass bin from the residuals distribution in Figure~\ref{fig:BcMCfitBin0}, otherwise the fit quality is satisfactory and the fitted shape is entirely consistent with the MC.

\subsubsection{Semi-leptonic Backgrounds}
\label{subsubsec:SemilepBkg}

The semi-leptonic background originates from few-body semi-leptonic \B-decays where in the limit of low energy neutrinos a hadron can fake a muon ($h\rightarrow\mu$): the main contributions to this background are the \BdPiMuNu and the \BsKMuNu decays. Also contributing to this background are the \LPMuNu (and its charge conjugate \AntiLPMuNu) decays, for which the mass distribution extends closer to the signal region. Together these semi-leptonic modes are expected to contribute significantly less than the second largest contribution to the total background (SS+SV decays), hence the BDT output bin content for these decays is expected to also be very low.

\textcolor{red}{Describe the procedure to get estimation of the semileptonic yields from the fake rates.}

With these few events expected to enter in the fit region we assume that the exponential (SS+SV) and Chebychev first degree polynomial (combinatorial) PDFs to absorb this contribution, so no explicit semi-leptonic component PDF is added to the default fit model. This assumption is taken into account in the evaluation of the fit systematic uncertainties in section~\ref{subsec:SystUncertaintySimultaneousFit}. To evaluate the impact of this fit systematic by including the semileptonic PDF in the full model, a functional form for this component is needed. The semi-leptonic decays are modelled by an exponential plus a gaussian tail as shown in equation~\ref{eq:expGaussTail}
\begin{eqnarray}
  F(x) = f \cdot E(x; \alpha) + (1 - f) \cdot G(x; \mu, \sigma)  
       = f \cdot \text{exp}(\alpha x) + (1 - f) \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \text{exp}\left[-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right]
  \label{eq:expGaussTail}
\end{eqnarray}
, where $\alpha$ is the exponential constant, $f$ is the fraction of the exponetial with respect to the Gaussian, and the $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are resepctively the mean and sigma of the Gaussian.

To validate the chosen PDF for the semi-leptonic background we fit the \B-candidate VTX mass distribution from the merged exclusive MC samples for the three modes with the PDF in equation~\ref{eq:expGaussTail} using a loosened selection: all final selections apart from the muon quality requirements and passing the online trigger are applied to the merged MC sample to allow for more statistics necessary for a good quality fit and convergence. Loosening the selections on the semi-leptonic MC to extract the shape parameters for this background is justified because this PDF is only used in evaluation of the the fit systematics and is not intended for the final fit on data. The loosened selection MC fit results are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:semilepMCfitLoose} with the fitted PDF in all BDT output bins satisfactory reproducing the shape of the MC sample as can be seen from the residual distributions. With enough statistics the fit quality is also satisfactory with $p-value$ > 0.05 in each bin of the BDT output. These fitted shape parameters are used to fix the shape parameters for the fit in the full selection merged semi-leptonic MC sample as an extra step of validation: the shape from the loosened selection MC fit must be compatible with the full selection fit to validate this approach.

\subsection{Non-Resonant Background Fits to MC and Data Sidebands}
\label{subsubsec:FitsMcDataSB}

First we validate the combination of the chosen functional forms in section~\ref{subsubsec:SSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB}, after which we validate the additional constraints on the exponential and polynomial slopes (arising from introducing the BDT-simultaneous fit strategy) in section~\ref{subsubsec:SimultaneousSSSVplusCOMBcombinedFitMCandDataSB}.
