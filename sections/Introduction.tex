\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
This note provides supporting material for the analysis of the rare \bstomumu and \bdtomumu decays using for the first time the Full Run 2 dataset collected by the ATLAS Experiment between 2015 and 2018.
The primary aim of the analysis is to use this dataset to make the most precise measurement to date of the branching fraction of these decays with ATLAS. 

Being heavily suppressed in the Standard Model, precise measurements of the branching fractions of these decays serve as powerful probes of potential new physics. 
The most up to date (\textcolor{red}{check}) theoretical predictions of the branching fractions are: 

\begin{itemize}
    \item \BRbstomumu = $(3.66 \pm 0.14) \times 10^{-9}$ \cite{Beneke:2019slt}  
    \item \BRbdtomumu = $(1.03 \pm 0.05) \times 10^{-10}$ \cite{Beneke:2019slt}  
\end{itemize}

% previous measurements 
The first evidence for the \bstomumu decay came from a combination of measurements of the 2011-2012 data collected by the \lhcb and \cms experiments~\cite{CMS:2014xfa}.  
Since then, observation of this decay has been confirmed by these experiments and by \atlas individually (\atlas~\cite{ATLAS:2018cur}, \cms~\cite{CMS:2019bbr}, \lhcb~\cite{LHCb:2017rmj}). 
The most precise measurement of the branching fraction comes from the combination of the ATLAS, CMS and LHCb partial Run 2 results~\cite{CMS-PAS-BPH-20-003} and is equal to ($2.69^{+0.37}_{-0.35}) \times 10^{-9}$, differing by 2.4 standard deviations from the theoretical prediction. 
The \cms and \lhcb experiments have also performed measurements of \BRbstomumu using their respective Full Run 2 datasets, yielding values of $(3.83^{+0.44}_{-0.41} \times 10^{-9})$~\cite{CMS:2022mgd} and $(3.09^{+0.48}_{-0.44} \times 10^{-9})$~\cite{LHCb:2021vsc} respectively. 
The statistical uncertainty in both of these Full Run 2 measurements and in the Run 1 combination is the dominant contribution to the total uncertainty. 

The decay \bdtomumu is yet to be observed, being approximately ten times smaller than \bstomumu in terms of expected yield.  
The most stringent upper limit on \BRbdtomumu at 95\% confidence level is $1.9 \times 10^{-9}$, around twice the predicted branching fraction, coming separately from the Full Run 2 \cms measurement and the ATLAS-CMS-LHCb Run 1 combination.   

% refer to previous analyses
The analysis strategy of the ATLAS Full Run 2 measurement documented here follows closely that of the previous ATLAS analysis using just the 2015-2016 data (paper~\cite{ATLAS:2018cur}, Internal Note~\cite{Cerri:2311593}), here onwards referred to as the Partial Run 2 (PR2) analysis.  
An overview of the strategy is provided in the next section. 
Aside from the approximately four times larger dataset, the main differences with respect to the PR2 analysis are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item In 2017 and 2018, topological triggers are used instead of the comparatively simpler triggers used in 2015-2016.  
    \item Improvements to the fit model and uncertainty evaluation of the \bptojpsik reference channel.  
    \item An improved Boosted Decision Tree (BDT) for reducing the combinatorial background, making use of custom track isolation variables. A dedicated cut and binning optimization on the BDT score for the FR2 dataset is also performed. 
\end{itemize}
Each of these is covered in detail in this note, as are aspects of the analysis more closely aligned with the PR2 methodology.
